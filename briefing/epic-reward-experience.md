# Epic: Reward Experience

## Outcome we are designing for

When a child completes all their daily tasks, they receive a short, positive Korean-language reward moment that celebrates their effort, reinforces their connection to Korean culture, and motivates them to return the next day.

The reward is not just a demo trick. It is a core part of the product vision — supporting motivation, emotional completion, and repeated engagement.

---

## Primary user in this epic

**Child user.**

The child aged 4–6 has just completed all their Hangul matching tasks for the day. They need a moment of celebration that feels earned, culturally connected, and worth coming back for.

---

## User stories

- As a child, I want something exciting to happen when I finish all my tasks, so that I feel proud of completing my routine.
- As a child, I want the reward to feel like a treat in Korean, so that I feel connected to Korean culture.
- As a child, I want to unlock and open a reward after my routine, so that I have something to look forward to each day.
- As a parent, I want the reward to be short and positive, so that it does not distract from the learning goal or become more appealing than the routine itself.
- As a parent, I want the reward to be culturally appropriate and Korean-language related, so that it reinforces the learning purpose.

---

## Jobs To Be Done

- When I finish all my daily tasks, I want a celebration moment, so I feel motivated to complete my routine again tomorrow.
- When I see the reward, I want it to feel special and Korean, so I feel connected to the language I am learning.

## Experience notes

### Completion state

- After the child matches all tasks correctly, the app shows a completion state before the reward
- Completion state should be clearly celebratory but calm
- Should include a visual celebration (e.g. confetti, stars, animation)
- Should include Korean language — e.g. a congratulations phrase or completion message
- The child then has the option to unlock the reward

### Reward unlock

- A reward button or reward card becomes active after the completion state
- The child taps or opens it to see the reward
- The reward is distinct from the completion state — it is a separate moment

### Reward format for Phase 1

**A mock video card is confirmed as the Phase 1 reward.**

The mock video card should:

- Represent a short Korean story or culturally connected Korean content
- Look like a video card even if the actual video is static or simulated for Phase 1
- Include a Korean title, a thumbnail image, and ideally a short Korean phrase
- Feel like a treat — something the child might want to watch
- Be child-friendly and culturally connected

The source note refers to "Korean Story related App" visible in the sketch — this aligns with the mock video card concept.

The reward card example in the card library data uses: 선물 (present) with the prompt 선물 받을 시간이야 (time to receive a present).

### Reward principles

- Short — the reward should not be longer than necessary
- Positive — warm and celebratory in tone
- Korean-language related — should expose the child to at least one Korean phrase or word
- Child-friendly — appropriate for children aged 4–6
- Culturally connected — should feel like a window into Korean culture, not a generic cartoon reward
- Not too distracting from the learning goal — the reward completes the loop, it does not replace learning

### Do not use

- Copyrighted cartoon content unless explicitly approved
- Unlicensed video content
- Anything unreviewed by a Korean-speaking adult

### Key states

- **Completion state:** All tasks matched — celebration shown, reward teased
- **Reward locked state:** Reward visible but not yet active (before final task is completed)
- **Reward unlocked state:** Reward becomes active, child can tap to open it
- **Reward open state:** Mock video card revealed with Korean content
- **Reward dismissed state:** Child closes reward and returns to the routine screen or completion screen

---

## Rules and constraints

- Reward is unlocked only after all daily tasks are correctly matched
- The reward should be short — it is a moment, not a full activity
- All Korean phrases in the reward must be reviewed by a Korean-speaking adult before use
- Do not use copyrighted cartoon content unless approved
- Do not use unlicensed video or image content in the reward
- The reward must be culturally appropriate and child-friendly
- The mock video card content for Phase 1 should be simple — a static or near-static card is acceptable as a simulation

---

## Phase 1 scope

- Completion state shown after all tasks are matched
- Reward card becomes active after completion state
- Child taps to open the reward
- Mock video card revealed — Korean-themed, child-friendly, culturally connected
- Korean phrase or word visible in the reward moment
- Reward is dismissible — child can return to the routine screen

---

## Later-phase opportunities

- Multiple reward types (story card, short video clip, Korean phrase of the day, mini game)
- Parent-controlled reward settings — parent can choose what type of reward the child receives
- A growing reward collection the child can revisit
- Weekly or monthly special rewards for streak completion
- AI-assisted reward content suggestions (with parent review)
- Cultural Korean content library (folktales, songs, short animations)
- Parent-approved mini content selection
